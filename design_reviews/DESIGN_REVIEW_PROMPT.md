**Nothing you say in the chat will be read or kept. Your review exists only if it is a
file in your directory.** Make a directory

    design_reviews/<YYYY-MM-DD>_<YourModel-Version>/

at the top of this repository, dated the day you start and named after yourself

## 1. What it is

The op-amp of Lab 5L, built from six 2N3904 / 2N3906 transistors in SOT-23 on a
100 x 100 mm two-layer board whose **silkscreen is the schematic**: the white wires are
the copper, each transistor sits at the centre of its own drawn symbol, dashed boxes
name the stages as the book names them, and 18 wire loops take a scope probe. Three
2-way DIP switches select the book's variants (TAIL: current sink or 10 k resistor;
COMPENSATION: 100 pF + 1 k, or the bare 100 pF; FEEDBACK: x11, follower, or open), the
inputs and the output are on BNCs and screw terminals, and +/-15 V comes in on a
three-way terminal. It is for first-year students who have done most of Chapter 4 of
the book, as a one-session bridge to Chapter 6, and afterwards as a bare op-amp in
their own circuits in Labs 6L to 9L. JLCPCB assembles it, through-hole parts
included, from the files in `out/opamp/` as they stand.

Everything was generated by the Python under `scripts/` and `sim/` from a conversation
with Claude (`CLAUDE_CODE_CHAT.md`, ten prompts); nothing was drawn by hand. Read the
generators as well as their output.

## 2. The claim to break

"A class of first-year students can do Lab 5L on this board stage by stage, use it as a
bare op-amp in Labs 6L to 9L, and it survives the mistakes they will make; JLCPCB will
assemble it from `out/opamp/` as it stands; and nothing on the board or in its
documents is wrong." The board's own numbers, each of which the build checks:

- ERC 0; DRC 0 at every severity with schematic parity, with KiCad's silkscreen rules
  on (0.8 mm minimum text, 0.15 mm silk clearance);
- 110 circuit assertions pass against the exported netlist; the sheet has no text
  overlaps; the fab package passes its checks (gerber layers and extent, drill files,
  BOM against CPL, each LCSC code's JLCPCB description against the value and package,
  stock, the QR code decodes, the keyed parts, the assembly drawings fill a Letter
  page, no schematic text crosses its frame); JLCPCB's own drawing of every part,
  placed by the upload CPL, lands on our pads (posts, polarity and the connectors'
  cable faces included), and its symbol names the transistors' pins as ours;
- 35 numbers quoted in the documents match the build;
- the simulation (`sim/results.json`, generic 2N3904 / 2N3906 models, 3 pF of stray on
  the three high-impedance nodes, the rails fed through modelled B5819Ws from +/-15 V):
  rails +/-14.83 V, tail 1.36 mA, Q3 1.28 mA, open-loop gain 126 at rest, stage gains
  11.4 and 32 (11.0 through the resting push-pull), x11 amplifier gain 10.1, follower
  phase margin 55 degrees (-38 without the 1 k), x11 phase margin 119 degrees,
  unity-gain crossing 14 MHz, swing -13.8 to +12.9 V into the 11 k divider and
  +/-10.1 V into 1 k, short-circuit output current 46 mA through R17 with at most
  127 mW in Q4 or Q5, no ringing with 100 pF to 2.2 nF on OUT, follower offset
  -6.7 mV, null range +/-33 mV;
- 61 parts, all on the front: 53 SMT, 8 through-hole (three BNCs, four terminals, the
  trimmer); 4 M3 holes; 24 vias; 4 hops (crossings on the back layer); 39 BOM lines, 8
  JLCPCB Extended codes; 100 SMT and 27 through-hole joints a board; parts $7.79 a
  board; 2 assembled $78.62, 10 assembled $151.76.

## 3. What to attack

Each item names where to look. Disagreement is welcome; a numbered finding with a file
and line, or a reference designator and net, and a number with its source, is the most
useful form.

1. **The circuit against the book** (`scripts/gen_sch.py`, `out/opamp.net`,
   `docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md` section 1). Fig. 5L.15: the 2N3904 difference amplifier with
   its 1.5 k collector load on the IN+ side, the 2N3906 common-emitter gain stage
   (330 ohm, 12 k), the 100 pF Miller capacitor, the 2N3904 / 2N3906 push-pull, the
   10 k / 1 k divider whose tap X is fed back to IN-; Fig. 5L.10's current sink (62 k /
   10 k divider, 1 k emitter). Is every value and every connection the book's? Is Q3
   drawn and wired the right way up for a PNP? Does `check_circuit.py` assert
   everything that matters, and what does it not assert?
2. **What the board adds** (`docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md` section 2), each as a circuit:
   - the offset null, RV1 50 ohm between the emitters, wiper to the tail: the null
     range (the documents quote +/-33 mV; `scripts/parts.py` and `check_circuit.py`
     have their own figures), the pair's gain with the wiper at an end, what happens if
     the wiper opens or the ends short;
   - the input protection, R1 / R2 1 k 1206 and D1 / D2 anti-parallel between the
     bases: the worst case a student can produce (one input at +15 V and the other at
     -15 V, open loop), the current, the power in each 1206, the base-emitter reverse
     voltage on the off transistor, whether the clamp ever interferes with normal use
     (open-loop exercises, the comparator of Lab 8L);
   - R14, the 1 k in series with the 100 pF: the right-half-plane zero argument, with
     the 330 ohm emitter degeneration, and whether the switch positions do what the
     HANDBOOK says;
   - the output protection, R17 330 ohm 2512 from the push-pull's emitters to OUT,
     inside the loop: OUT shorted to ground, to +15 V and to -15 V (the power in Q4 or
     Q5 in a SOT-23 on these pads, and in R17), whether 330 ohm is the right value
     (the sweep in `docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md` section 2), what it costs in swing and loop
     gain, its effect on stability with a cable on OUT, and whether the HANDBOOK's
     claims about shorts are true;
   - D3 / D4 B5819W in the rails: polarity, drop, what a reversed or a single supply
     does, what +30 V on one terminal does;
   - the LEDs and bypass capacitors.
3. **Stability and the simulation** (`sim/opamp_sim.py`, `sim/results.json`). The
   models are generic (Bf 300 / 200, Cjc 4 to 4.5 pF, fT about 300 MHz); check them
   against onsemi's MMBT3904 / MMBT3906 datasheets. Then the questions the simulation
   has not been asked:
   - a 10x scope probe (10 to 15 pF) on TP8 (Q3 C) or TP6 (Q2 C), as the exercises in
     HANDBOOK section 8 require: does the follower still have margin, with and without
     the 1 k?
   - a metre of coax on OUT (about 100 pF) with FEEDBACK 2, and with a student's own
     follower wired from the OUT terminal: does it ring at any output level (the
     simulation says no, from 100 pF to 2.2 nF, with R17 inside the loop)?
   - both FEEDBACK sliders on (R10 shorted);
   - the x11 amplifier with 2 V peak in, the slew rate, the crossover dead band at
     TP8.
4. **The silkscreen as a schematic.** Read `docs/images/opamp-silk-top.png` and the
   `docs/history/2026-10-24-r4-*.png` close-ups as a student would, with no other
   document: is every connection a junction dot where three wires meet and a hop (a
   small arc) where two cross without meeting? Is every transistor's B, C and E on the
   right pad, the arrow on the emitter, NPN and PNP distinguishable, the hairline from
   each pad to its lead where the copper really goes? Is any label ambiguous,
   misleading, abbreviated or beside the wrong thing? Do the switch legends, the box
   names and the loop names match `docs/HANDBOOK.md`? Grounds point down (except on
   the -15 V side), rails are lines, +/- are written as such.
5. **Copper and board** (`hardware/opamp.kicad_pcb`, `scripts/gen_pcb.py`,
   `scripts/layout.py`). Copper follows the drawn wires and DRC's parity says every
   net is complete: check the hops (a back-layer segment between two vias under a silk
   arc), the ground plane's integrity where hops and the 32 vias cut it, the 0.35 mm
   tracks for 20 mA, clearances to the M3 holes and to the BNC flanges, the three
   fiducials (`layout.py FIDUCIALS`), the custom footprints in
   `hardware/lib/opamp.pretty/` (BNC_KYWE_RightAngle, the 3362P, the KF301 terminals,
   the DSIC02 switch, the RH-5019 loop) against their drawings, and the SOT-23 pad
   assignment (KiCad's `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23`, pads 1 = B, 2 = E, 3 = C) against
   the MMBT3904 / MMBT3906 datasheets.
6. **The JLCPCB package** (`out/opamp/`, `scripts/parts.py JLC_ROTATION`,
   `out/opamp/PLACEMENT_CHECKS.txt`, `docs/MANUFACTURING.md`). The CPL rotations
   marked *predicted* were derived from JLCPCB's land patterns and never seen in a
   preview: SOT-23 +180 (six transistors), the DSIC02 switches +270, the KF301
   terminals and the 3362P at 0, the SOD-123 diodes at 0 (`python3 scripts/easyeda.py
   C20526` in your copy fetches their drawing). Check the polarised parts: D1-D4 and
   the LEDs. Does the DSIC02 switch connect pins 1-4 and 2-3 across the body, as the
   footprint and `Switch:SW_DIP_x02` assume? Does JLCPCB's Economic PCBA assemble the
   eight through-hole parts, or does the order need Standard PCBA, and what does the
   cost model in `scripts/gen_docs.py` assume (setup $8.00, stencil $1.50, THT setup
   $3.58, $3.00 per Extended code, per-joint fees)? Anything else that would stop
   their line or their importer.
7. **The documents as a lab handout.** `docs/HANDBOOK.md`: does every exercise work as
   described given the simulation; do the expected loop voltages follow from the
   circuit; is anything a first-year student would misread? Do the three documents
   agree with each other and with the board (the build checks 28 numbers; which
   numbers, counts and names does it not check)? `docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md` section 5 is
   the author's list of what could make a board dead on arrival: what is missing from
   it?
8. **What a student will do to it.** Supply reversed; +15 and -15 swapped; one rail
   only; 30 V between one rail and ground; a 20 V peak generator into an input; a
   scope's ground clip on a signal loop (it shorts that node to ground: TP11 is the
   +15 V rail); OUT tied to an input, to ground, to a rail; a 100 ohm load; the
   trimmer turned to an end; a DIP slider half way. Which of these damages the board,
   and what would prevent it at no cost to clarity?

## 4. The standard

The author follows a rules file, `CLAUDE_PCB.md`, that is not in this repository. Its
points that apply here, to be used as checks:

- every connector pin and every part labelled beside it, by what it does, in full
  words, never elsewhere by designator; connector names big, with their limits;
- nothing on the silkscreen overlapping anything; no text under 0.8 mm, no line under
  0.15 mm; nothing sits on a dashed box's edge;
- the silkscreen is the schematic: wires are copper, junction dots where three meet,
  hops where two cross; a transistor's symbol encloses its package;
- every LCSC code checked against its description and its land pattern; every
  rotation either read in a real preview or marked predicted and listed for the
  preview; polarised parts' marks in the fab notes;
- no hand soldering: JLCPCB fits everything, through-hole included;
- three fiducials; no front copper pour under a drawing;
- one source of truth for every fact (`scripts/parts.py`, `scripts/layout.py`), and
  every number in a document checked against the build;
- a reproducible, deterministic build from one command, relative paths only;
- the most likely causes of a dead board listed, each with what catches it.

## 7. The report: `REPORT.md` in your directory

It must stand alone: the author reads it without the chat, beside the other reviews.

1. **Verdict**, one paragraph: order as it is; order after named fixes; do not order.
   Say how sure you are.
2. **Findings**, most severe first, numbered, first as a table (number, severity,
   where, one line) and then each in full:
   - severity: **blocker** (the board cannot be made, or will not work, or is unsafe
     in a teaching lab); **major** (a feature or an exercise is likely to fail, a
     part is outside its ratings, a document teaches something wrong); **minor**
     (works, but worse than it should); **nit**;
   - where: file and line, or reference designator and net;
   - what is wrong, and what you expected;
   - the evidence: a calculation, a datasheet's figure and page, a simulation or DRC
     or render you made and kept in your directory;
   - the fix you propose, as a change to the generator where that applies;
   - how sure you are.
3. **Section 3's questions**, each answered by number, with the number or the
   simulation that answers it.
4. **What you checked and found right**, so the next round need not repeat it.
5. **What you could not check**, and why.
6. **Publishing**: anything that should not be in a public repository (copyrighted
   text or drawings, personal information, absolute paths, keys).
7. **Patches**, if any, as files in your directory, listed here.

Write the report as you go. If you are stopped, a half-finished report beats none.
